# Limiter 0.3 DSP design and qualification

Original OpenFilter code and graphics. FabFilter's panel/workflow is a reference,
not a reconstruction of its private algorithms or a claim of sonic equivalence.
Bitwig testing and real-material listening are assigned to the user; numerical
qualification is recorded in status.md.

## Research

- [FabFilter advanced settings](https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-l/using/advancedsettings):
  fast and sustained stages, lookahead, timing and independent channel linking.
  OpenFilter uses independently designed gain laws for this division of controls.
- [FabFilter oversampling](https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-l/using/oversampling):
  nonlinear processing at higher rates, aliasing, filter ringing and CPU tradeoffs.
- [FabFilter true-peak limiting](https://www.fabfilter.com/help/pro-l/using/truepeaklimiting):
  input inter-sample peaks and peaks generated by limiting are separate problems.
- [ITU-R BS.1770-5, Annex 2](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770-5-202311-I/en):
  reconstruction and finite sampling-grid under-read. Our filters are original
  windowed-sinc designs, not the example coefficient table.
- Giannoulis, Massberg and Reiss, *Digital Dynamic Range Compressor Design—A
  Tutorial and Analysis*, JAES 60(6), 2012, is envelope-design background.
  The [author URL](https://eecs.qmul.ac.uk/~josh/documents/2012/GiannoulisMassbergReiss-dynamicrangecompression-JAES2012.pdf)
  redirected during research; no implementation code is imported.
- [libebur128 1.2.6 API](https://github.com/jiixyj/libebur128/blob/v1.2.6/ebur128/ebur128.h)
  provides a separate development-time true-peak cross-check. It is not a plugin
  dependency and no library code is copied into OpenFilter.

## Signal path

Modern styles: input gain → 4× interpolation → transient/sustained gain stages →
decimation → narrow Nyquist band limit → sample/reconstructed-peak guards →
ceiling/unity trim → aligned bypass → final-output peak meter.

ModernEngine owns the gain computer, Engine owns routing, delay, resampling and
protection, and LegacyEngine retains the old gain equations. DSP has no CLAP/UI
dependency. Storage is instance-owned and fixed; parameter updates and processing
do not allocate, lock, access files, log or call GUI code. All filters and both
engines stay warm during style/protection automation. Parameters slew per sample,
including at the internal processing rate. Host events remain at their sample offsets.

## Audio-path oversampling and bandwidth

Clean/Punch/Dense run at 4× up to 192 kHz host rate, 2× above 192 kHz through
384 kHz, and 1× above 384 kHz. This bounds internal processing at 768 kHz, the
same capacity limit tested by the original engine. The mode is fixed on activation,
not an automatable setting; the footer reports it when space permits.

Two original linear-phase half-band FIR stages use 257 and 33 taps (Kaiser beta
10). Every other tap is zero, the central tap is 0.5, and symmetric pairs share
multiplications. Each interpolation phase has unity DC gain. The second stage is
omitted at 2×, and both at 1×. Upsampling, nonlinear gain computation and
anti-alias downsampling all process actual audio; this is not merely an
oversampled detector. Round-trip delays are 136, 128 and 0 host samples.

A 257-tap Kaiser low-pass after decimation has cutoff 0.475 × host sample rate
(0.95 × Nyquist) and delay 128 samples. Its transition is above 20 kHz at the
lowest normal music rate, 44.1 kHz. Exact-Nyquist alternating samples otherwise
sit on a half-band filter's boundary; sharply gating them can generate growing
reconstruction ringing as the measurement kernel grows. The band limit fixes
that known failure without clipping the waveform. It remains active in modern
styles with True Peak off. In Legacy it is active only with True Peak on; with
protection off an equally delayed wire preserves the old audio path. Both paths
are warm, and the existing protection ramp crossfades them.

Linear-phase filters introduce a finite impulse response and pre/post ringing.
Below-threshold modern audio therefore no longer nulls against a simple delayed
wire. The qualification tool measures the actual rendered impulse response,
DC gain, 20 Hz–20 kHz deviation, Nyquist rejection, symmetry and latency.

## Transient and sustained gain computation

At the internal rate, D is the fixed maximum 5 ms lookahead, H is 26 ms retention,
and L is one of six lookahead horizons: 0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 ms. The stage
reads driven audio at n−D+L and requires gain `r = 1 / max(1, abs(x))`.
A causal minimum over L+H+1 samples constrains an instant-attack release envelope:
`e[n] = min(minimum[n], 1 − a × (1 − e[n−1]))`. A positive moving average of
L+1 envelope samples gives gain. Every averaged constraint includes the output
sample, so the average remains sample-safe. A final rounding bound precedes
linking. Adjacent path gains blend continuously for intermediate Lookahead values;
this is envelope blending, not a variable audio delay. At zero lookahead, fast
gain changes still create distortion; oversampling reduces rather than abolishes it.

The 26 ms hold covers a half-cycle at 20 Hz. A 10 ms prototype produced roughly
−36 dB sine residual at 20 Hz; the longer hold improved the previous build to
−77 dB in that test. Legacy retains its original 10 ms hold.

The sustained stage follows held attenuation in dB. Attack/Release are one-pole
63.2% time constants. Auto Release uses a 250 ms attenuation average to lengthen
release by up to 4× at 12 dB average attenuation. Transient and sustained linking
independently add attenuation toward the more attenuated channel. Their final
gains combine by minimum, not multiplication.

| Style | Fast recovery | Sustained demand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean | 40 ms | 1× |
| Punch | 8 ms | 0.75× |
| Dense | 80 ms | 1.2× |
| Legacy | Original engine | Original engine |

Policies slew over 10 ms. Legacy ignores Lookahead, Attack and Release Link;
those controls are visibly unavailable. These are original envelope voicings.

## Post-decimation protection and metering

Decimation and band limiting can create new peaks. Two warm guards therefore
observe the filtered output: one constrains sample peaks even with True Peak off,
the other constrains reconstructed peaks. They use linked stereo detection,
3 ms positive gain averaging, 10 ms hold and 100 ms recovery. The true-peak guard
uses a 0.3 dB allowance only near the ceiling; quiet audio is not constantly trimmed.
There is no final waveform clipper hiding filter overshoots.

Reconstruction cascades 513-, 33- and 17-tap half-band interpolators to 8×.
Their full support is 266 host samples; the guard delay includes that support
plus its gain-averaging window. Symmetric, zero-tap-aware polyphase evaluation
replaces 0.2's expensive eight long direct convolutions. A separate warm detector
measures final output, including bypass/comparison. The UI selects true-peak or
sample-peak readings with True Peak; it retains the existing 400 ms peak decay.
The reduction history accounts for downsampling/band-limit delay and reports
stage attenuation; it is not a sample-by-sample input/output ratio through FIR ringing.

Independent checks use 32× reconstruction with kernels from 8193 through 65537
taps, 64× reconstruction for meter checks, analytical sine amplitudes, and
libebur128 1.2.6. Its short Hann detector under-reads some high-frequency
calibration sines; analytical amplitudes and the denser reconstruction remain
the accuracy references, and the external deviations are reported explicitly.
These are specified finite reconstructions, not a guarantee for
every possible DAC or lossy codec, nor a formal BS.1770 certification.

## Bounded processing work

Sliding-minimum queues discard a fully superseded tail in constant time. A
partial replacement performs at most four linear pops then a binary search of
the sorted remaining queue. A single new peak no longer triggers thousands of
pops. At the one-sample update cadence, expiry retires at most one time entry.
Timestamp comparisons use unsigned modular differences well below 2^32 samples;
wrap and long-queue behavior have independent regression tests. Unchanged
coefficients/held levels are cached. FIR evaluation uses four independent
accumulators without unsafe floating-point compiler options.

The benchmark preallocates everything and separately measures callback wall time
and thread CPU time, with tones, stereo overload, silence, long queue replacement
and parameter automation. Wall-time scheduling stalls remain possible on a
non-real-time desktop; measured CPU headroom is not a promise about DAW scheduling.

## State, sound and latency compatibility

All twelve parameter IDs, defaults, ranges, enum values and schema 2 stay stable.
Schema 1 loads as Legacy/True Peak off. Invalid counts/IDs/values/checksums are
rejected before publication; active loads retain bounded handoff and clear
modulation. Saved states contain parameters, not audio histories.

**0.3 is an intentional sound revision for existing modern-style states:** they
now use oversampling and band limiting. It does not silently claim bit-identical
0.2 modern recall. Legacy/True Peak off retains its gain equations and delayed
wire, apart from floating-point rounding. Legacy/True Peak on gets the new safety
filter and detector. Preserve the previous binary if exact 0.2 recall is needed.

Latency is `ceil(rate×.005) + resamplingDelay + 128 + ceil(rate×.003) + 266`:
914 samples / 19.04 ms at 48 kHz, versus 640 in 0.2 and 240 in 0.1. It stays fixed
through all parameter changes and bypass. Legacy is padded to the same delay.
The CLAP tail includes the additional resampling/band-limit impulse-response
extent, so offline rendering does not stop at the group-delay sample.

Output-time ceiling/unity/bypass ramps still apply at the event's output sample.
Internal gain automation travels through the revised pipeline. Hosts must refresh
latency compensation, and earlier timed automation must be checked after upgrading.

## Release qualification

The numerical gates include independent batch equations, actual audio and FIR
transfer, high-rate convergence, long-kernel/external peak checks, sample-offset
partition invariance, allocation guards, state migration, sanitizers, CLAP fuzzing
and native UI gestures. Exact results and limitations are in status.md.

The user will perform Bitwig recall/automation/export and real-material listening.
LUFS metering and dither remain optional unimplemented features. Neither is a
prerequisite for using a limiter with external delivery metering/dither.
